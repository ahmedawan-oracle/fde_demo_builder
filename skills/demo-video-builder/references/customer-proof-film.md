# Customer proof films: story first, product proof second

Use this treatment when the brief asks for a prospect-specific film rather than a feature walkthrough. It applies
the production-film pipeline to a customer world, then hands the story to real product evidence.

## Learn craft from a reference, never copy content

Run `tools/ref_study.py measure <reference.mp4>` and record only craft measurements: shot-length range, cut rhythm,
luma/contrast bands, framing, camera movement, and the ratio of story footage to product proof. Then run
`tools/ref_study.py report`; it generates `REFERENCES.md`, which is not hand-edited. Do not reuse a reference's
logos, copy, diagrams, music, claims, or customer data. Write the new film from the current brief and verified
product evidence.

## Treatment

Use the `customer-story` scaffold and divide the film into three acts:

1. **Customer world and decision.** Establish the industry, the decision that is currently hard, and one measurable
   consequence. Use approved customer assets or licensed/generated b-roll; do not imitate a product screen.
2. **Data-to-agent explanation.** In a few animated beats, show the real architecture or semantic relationships that
   make the decision possible. Every number is in `claims.json`; every system relationship comes from the approved
   design or implementation. Mark synthetic data clearly.
3. **Product proof and outcome.** Hand off to real full-screen product footage. Show a small connected sequence of
   actions and results, then close on the decision and next step. Each spoken result links to a receipt or visible
   product state.

For a 90-150 second film, budget roughly 15-25 seconds for acts 1-2, 65-110 seconds for product proof, and 10-15
seconds for the close. Longer films may expand each act, but product proof remains the majority. A short film should
show fewer complete proofs, not montage a long capability list.

## Visual and sound language

- Derive a contrast-checked skin from the approved brand kit. Keep product footage in its recorded colours.
- Use one recurring visual idea from the customer domain, one architecture/ontology sequence, and no more than
  three transition techniques. Each product beat establishes the whole screen before a purposeful push-in.
- Keep an instrumental bed across most of the film, carved beneath narration. Place restrained SFX on major act
  changes and evidence landings, outside the spoken-word guard window.
- Use clear narration that explains why the action matters. The screen carries evidence; it does not become a
  background for reading the interface aloud.

## Localized captions

When the audience needs another language, translate reviewed caption groups rather than the narration script's
individual words. First render the current groups, then emit a source-locked translation template:

```bash
python tools/captions_ass.py out/caption_groups.json \
  --emit-translation-template captions_<lang>.json --language <lang> --direction rtl \
  --font "Noto Sans Arabic"
```

Fill its `lines` arrays without changing `source` or `source_sha256`:

```json
{
  "_language": "ar",
  "_direction": "rtl",
  "_font": "Noto Sans Arabic",
  "groups": {
    "cg-0": {
      "source": "Original narration cue",
      "source_sha256": "generated by the tool",
      "lines": ["First translated line", "Second translated line"]
    }
  }
}
```

Set `film.json` to use the translation and a single caption lane:

```json
{
  "captions": {
    "config": "scenes/captions.json",
    "burn": false,
    "ass": true,
    "vtt": true,
    "translation": "captions_<lang>.json",
    "fonts_dir": "assets/fonts"
  },
  "exports": ["burn_ass", "share_pack"]
}
```

`build_film.py` refuses a translation with `burn:true` or word overrides, generates the localized ASS/SRT/VTT from
the same cue windows, and `burn_ass` creates the localized master without English captions underneath it. A changed
source cue, unknown group, missing group, more than two lines, or overlong line is a finding, so regenerate the
template after narration changes rather than trusting positional ids.

The translator or approved translation provider, glossary, and review status belong in the media ledger. Localize
product and business terms consistently; preserve numbers exactly. Use `tools/fonts_localize.py --local` for a font
that covers any localized text rendered in the HTML scene. For the ASS burn, place the licensed `.ttf`, `.otf`, or
`.ttc` files under `captions.fonts_dir`; the exporter passes that directory directly to libass. Record every font's
licence in the media ledger, inspect mixed right-to-left text/product names/numbers, and review the burn at delivery
size. Do not send confidential narration or customer data to a translation service without approval.

## Proof gates

- `STORYBOARD.md` labels every beat `recorded` or `recreated`; no placeholder ships.
- A story claim has a `claims.json` entry and a later product/evidence payoff, or it is removed.
- The product section shows complete actions and readable outcomes, with dead waits removed.
- Localized captions cover every shown cue, fit in two lines, preserve figures and product names, and are reviewed
  by an approved speaker or translator.
- Reference measurements guide rhythm and contrast only; `ref_study.py lint` passes.
- Picture, privacy, audio, credit, evidence, and whole-cut review gates pass before export.
